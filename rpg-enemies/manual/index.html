1 Overview

RPG Enemies is built on the TinnyStudios Utility AI framework, which comes included. It ships eight action-RPG enemy archetypes that notice, chase, surround and fight the player: a camp soldier, a slow bruiser, a fast duelist, an archer, a mage, a flying swarm, a pop-up shooter and a two-phase boss. Everything they do is a utility action scored by considerations, so you tune them by editing data, not code, and you can watch every decision in the AI Debug View.

What is in the box:

  • Eight enemy prefabs (Prefabs/Enemies) built from more than twenty reusable actions (chase, circle, flank, guard, dodge, flee, take cover, teleport, summon, heal, hide, throw objects, pick up weapons, ...) and 26 attacks defined as data (Data/Attacks).
  • Combat rules shared by everyone: health and poise (stagger), teams, blocking, parrying, guard breaks, knockback, projectiles that can be reflected, weapons that can be dropped and picked up, throwable props and explosive barrels.
  • Senses and awareness: sight cones, hearing, an awareness meter that fills before an enemy becomes alert, memory of where the player was last seen, sleeping enemies, alarms that wake a whole camp.
  • Group coordination: attack tokens so only a few enemies attack at once, surround slots so groups spread out, alerts.
  • Difficulty profiles (Data/Difficulty): four levels that change reaction time, damage, combos and more without touching the AI.
  • Wave encounters (EnemySpawner): portals, waves, barriers and events.
  • Feedback system: sounds, particles, camera shake and hit-stop for every gameplay moment, resolved from per-character profiles and per-attack cues, with placeholders until you add your own assets.
  • Animation tooling: one shared base Animator for every character, Override Controllers generated from a folder of clips, an editable clip mapping with six spare slots and named states of your own for new moves (kept in your folder, so an update keeps them), hit-frame detection, stride measurement, and a Swap Model tool that puts your own character on any enemy.
  • Weapon tools: line a weapon up in the hand with handles, check it through an attack, resize it (its reach follows), give an enemy another weapon, or make one from your own model.
  • A demo scene with a zone per archetype and an example player (Demo).

1.2 Installation

  1. Import the package. It brings the Utility AI core with it, in Assets/TinnyStudios/UtilityAI.

  2. On the first load of an editor session, RPG Enemies: Package Dependencies checks the project manifest and offers to install anything missing through the Package Manager (AI Navigation is required, Post Processing optional).   4. Open Demo/Scenes/RPGEnemiesDemo.unity and press Play. Walk into the camp or press 1-7 to jump to a zone.
  5. To put enemies in your own game, follow Quick Start.

1.6 Known limits

  • The art ships for the Built-in Render Pipeline; URP needs the one-click conversion, HDRP is not supported (see Render Pipelines).
  • The demo scripts and the AI Debug View read input through DemoInput (works with the Input Manager and the Input System package); the enemies themselves read no input.
  • Ground enemies need a NavMesh. Without one they stand still (the flying Swarm does not need one).
  • The Boss and the Heavy Melee use the same NavMesh as everyone else but are wider (about 1 m and 0.7 m radius), so give them room: they cannot follow the player through gaps a NavMesh baked for a 0.5 m agent still allows.

2 Quick Start

Five minutes from an empty scene to enemies that hunt your player. Nothing here needs code: your player only needs a way to move (any controller) and two components.

2.1 Your first enemy

1. Bake a NavMesh

Ground enemies walk with Unity's NavMeshAgent, so the level needs a NavMesh (AI Navigation package; the pack offers to install it, or use Tools ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Check Package Dependencies).

  1. Put your ground and walls under one parent object (a plane is enough to start).
  2. Add Navigation ▸ NavMesh Surface to that parent, set Collect Objects to Children and press Bake.

The enemy prefabs use the default Humanoid agent type. The Boss (about 1 m radius) and the Heavy Melee (0.7 m) walk on the same NavMesh, so keep corridors wider than 2 m where they go.

2. Drop in enemies

Drag any prefab from Prefabs/Enemies onto the NavMesh: Melee Soldier and Archer are a good first pair. Each prefab is complete: senses, movement, health, a FeedbackEmitter and an Actions child holding its behaviours. Nothing on it needs to be wired to your scene.

3. Make your player a target

Select your player object and Add Component ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Simple Combat Target, then press Add Health in its inspector (or add a Health yourself). The target keeps the Health on the Player team and registers the player as something the enemies can see, chase and hit. (With a damage system of your own instead of Health, see Integration.)

Two things the player object needs:

  • A collider (a CharacterController counts). Enemy hits are physics overlaps that look for the Health on the collider or one of its parents, and arrows fly with sphere casts.
  • Some way to move, so you can test. Any controller works; the component reads velocity from a CharacterController, a Rigidbody or the transform's movement (its Velocity Source) so archers and mages lead their shots.

Set Health ▸ Max Health to something you can survive with (the demo player has 150).

4. Play

Press Play and walk towards the enemies. An enemy that sees you fills an awareness meter (faster the closer you are), shows "?" when suspicious and "!" when alert, then chases, surrounds and attacks. Melee attacks wind up before they land, so you can step away; arrows are led at where you are going. Sprint and enemies hear you sooner; sneak up from behind and they notice later. Killing the player (or disabling the component) sends them back to searching, then to idling.

That is the whole integration for enemies that fight a player. Everything below is optional.

2.2 Next steps

Let enemies react to what the player does

Enemies dodge your swings, punish your heals, switch to guard-breakers when you block and answer dodge spam, but only if they know what you are doing. Tell the SimpleCombatTarget from your controller in any of three ways (no code needed for the last one):

  • set its IsAttacking, IsBlocking, IsDodging and IsHealing properties;
  • call NotifyAttackStarted(duration), NotifyDodge(duration) and NotifyHeal(duration);
  • assign your Animator on the component and tag your Animator states Attack, Block, Dodge and Heal.

Integration covers this, blocking and parrying, and how your attacks damage enemies (NewDamage + DealDamageInFront).

Coordinate a group

Enemies with no coordinator share one default GroupCoordinator (3 attackers at once, alerts within 25 m). For a camp or a room, parent its enemies under an object with a Group Coordinator:

Field Effect
Max Attackers How many enemies may attack at the same time. The rest circle and wait for an attack token. Difficulty profiles can add more: the group allows the most that any member's profile asks for.
Alert Radius When one member notices the player or is hit, every member within this distance becomes alert too.
Surround Slots Evenly spaced positions around the player; each member keeps its own, so groups spread out instead of stacking.

Enemies register with the coordinator on their nearest parent when they start (EnemyContext.Initialize), so spawned enemies join the group of the object they are parented to. To move one to another group later, call enemy.JoinGroup(group).

Change difficulty

Data/Difficulty holds Easy, Normal, Hard and Elite DifficultyProfile assets. Assign one to Enemy ▸ Difficulty on an enemy in your scene or on a Prefab Variant of your own (Authoring), or switch at runtime with enemy.ApplyDifficulty(profile) (the demo HUD's buttons do this for every enemy in Enemy.All, and for every enemy that starts later through Enemy.Spawned). A profile changes, without touching any action:

Profile Health Damage Speed Reaction time Awareness Dodge / Guard chance Wind-up Cooldown Extra combo hits Extra attackers
Easy ×0.75 ×0.6 ×0.9 0.45 s ×0.7 20% / 40% ×1.3 ×1.4 0 0
Normal ×1 ×1 ×1 0.25 s ×1 50% / 70% ×1 ×1 0 0
Hard ×1.4 ×1.3 ×1.1 0.15 s ×1.3 75% / 95% ×0.8 ×0.8 +1 +1
Elite ×2 ×1.6 ×1.2 0.08 s ×1.6 95% / 100% ×0.65 ×0.6 +2 +1

Reaction time is how often the AI re-checks its options mid-action. Dodge and guard chances multiply each Dodge and Guard action's own Skill. A profile can also tint the enemy (Tint, Tint Strength) so players can tell tiers apart. Make your own in your folder with Create ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Difficulty Profile, or by duplicating one of these, rather than changing the pack's four: a pack update puts them back.

See what they are thinking

Add an AI Debug View component (Scripts/Runtime/Debug) to any object in the scene. It works in the Editor and in Development Builds, and switches itself off in a release build unless you tick Show In Release Builds (the demo scene does). Press F1 (its Toggle Key) to draw, above every living enemy within Max Distance of the camera:

  • archetype, difficulty and [token] when it holds an attack token;
  • awareness state and meter, boss phase, STAGGERED, and GUARDING with the guard's remaining points;
  • the current action and the top three scoring actions with their scores.

Show Health Bars draws a bar over enemies that are hurt or engaged. This is the fastest way to tune: if an enemy never picks an action, its score tells you which consideration is zeroing it.

Spawn waves

Add an Enemy Spawner (Scripts/Runtime/Group) where an ambush should happen, and give it a GroupCoordinator on the same object so the spawned enemies coordinate.

Field Effect
Waves Each wave lists enemy prefabs with counts, a name and a delay before it starts. The next wave starts when every enemy of the current one is dead.
Trigger Radius The fight starts when a combat target comes this close. 0 = only when started from code (Begin).
Spawn Points / Spawn Radius Where enemies rise. Without points, random spots around the spawner.
Portal Time / Emerge Time A portal shows as a warning, then the enemy rises, invulnerable and inactive, over Emerge Time.
Alert On Spawn Enemies come out already hunting the player who triggered the fight.
Barriers Objects switched on during the fight and off when it is cleared, e.g. walls that lock the arena.
Rearm Delay Seconds after clearing before it can trigger again; below 0 it fires once.

If the player dies or leaves, the encounter resets and every spawned enemy is removed. Events gives you OnWaveStarted, OnWaveCleared, OnAllWavesCleared and OnEnemySpawned(Enemy) for health bars, quest counters or doors. From a script or a cutscene, Begin(target) starts the fight, Target is who it is against, and ResetEncounter() calls it off. GetSpawnPoint, CreateEnemy, RemoveEnemy and ResetEncounter are virtual, so a subclass can change where enemies appear, how they are made and removed, and what else resets with the fight, but enemies cannot be pooled yet: a dead enemy shuts its AI down for good and is destroyed, and an enemy sets itself up only once. The demo's spawn arena (zone 7) is a working example.

Give it sound and effects

Without any setup, hits, blocks, telegraphs and explosions show grey-box placeholders. To replace them, select your own Prefab Variant of an enemy (or an enemy in your scene), open its Feedback Emitter, press Create to make a profile (it asks where to save it, starting in a folder of yours), or Make Unique when it already uses one of the pack's, and drag audio clips and particle prefabs onto its moments. Integration explains the system and has recipes for one attack, one enemy type and every footstep at once.

3 Integration

How the enemies connect to your game. Everything is in the TinnyStudios.AIUtility.RPGEnemies namespace (assembly TinnyStudios.RPGEnemies). The Code Extending guide (RPGEnemies_CodeExtending.pdf) takes each of these connections further in C#.

Enemies need three things from you, and offer one back:

They need Provided by
Something to see, chase and read ICombatTarget, implemented for you by the SimpleCombatTarget component
Something to damage IDamageable, implemented by the Health component or by your own damage system
Somewhere to walk A NavMesh (see Quick Start)
They offer: a way for your attacks to hurt them IDamageable on every enemy, plus helpers on SimpleCombatTarget

The demo's Demo/Scripts/DemoPlayer.cs is a complete example of a player that uses all of this, including the optional guard, parry and projectile reflection. It is deliberately self-contained demo code: copy what you need into your own controller rather than building on it.

3.1 SimpleCombatTarget

Scripts/Runtime/Combat/SimpleCombatTarget.cs. Add it to your player next to a Health (its inspector offers Add Health while nothing on the object can be damaged), or next to your own damage system (see Your own damage system below). With either, it already makes enemies fight the player:

  • it implements ICombatTarget and registers with CombatTargets in OnEnable / unregisters in OnDisable, so disabling the component hides the player from every enemy;
  • it keeps the Health on the Player team (Reset, OnValidate and Awake all switch an Enemy team to Player; Neutral is left alone). On the Enemy team, enemies would ignore the player: they only fight a target their team may hurt;
  • it reports the player's velocity so ranged enemies lead their shots (Velocity Source: Auto uses the CharacterController, else the Rigidbody, else the transform's movement; Manual uses whatever you assign to Velocity);
  • it aims enemies at Aim Transform (e.g. a chest bone) or Aim Height above the root, which is also where line of sight is checked;
  • it makes footstep noise from your movement speed (Auto Footsteps: every Footstep Interval seconds while faster than Min Footstep Speed, heard from Walk Noise Radius, or Run Noise Radius above Run Speed).

Telling enemies what the player does

Enemies read four flags and a counter. Each changes real decisions:

Member Read by Effect on enemies
IsAttacking Target Is Attacking consideration The Agile Melee's Dodge scores while it is on. CombatEvents.AttackStarted (raised when it turns on) makes every enemy within the threat radius re-plan at once, so dodges and guards happen on the same frame as your swing.
IsBlocking Guard Breaker Bonus, Target Not Blocking Guard-breaking and unblockable attacks (Ground Slam, Heavy Charge, Boss Slam, Delayed Smash) score higher; the Agile Melee's Quick Slash and Lunge wait for you to lower the guard.
IsDodging Target Is Dodging flag, dodge counter Each rising edge counts one dodge.
IsHealing Punish Healing The Boss Charge scores double while you heal.
RecentDodgeCount Target Spams Dodge The Boss's Delayed Smash, an unblockable hit with a long wind-up, scores with the number of dodges in the last Dodge Memory (4) seconds.

Set them whichever way suits your controller; they combine, and the rising-edge logic (dodge counting, attack announcement) works the same for all three:

  1. Properties. Assign IsAttacking, IsBlocking, IsDodging, IsHealing every frame or on state changes. Setting one to false also ends a timed flag started by a helper. If your own damage script decides blocks, implement IGuardState (IsGuarding) on it too: IsBlocking then follows it, so the guard is set in one place.
  2. Timed helpers. NotifyAttackStarted(duration, threatRadius) warns enemies now and keeps IsAttacking on for duration seconds (0 = you manage the flag; threatRadius 0 = the inspector's Attack Threat Radius). NotifyDodge(duration) counts a dodge and keeps IsDodging on. NotifyHeal(duration) keeps IsHealing on.
  3. Animator tags. Assign your Animator on the component and give your attack, block, dodge and heal states the tags in Attack Tag, Block Tag, Dodge Tag and Heal Tag ("Attack", "Block", "Dodge", "Heal" by default). Any layer counts, including the state being transitioned into. No code at all.

With Announce Attacks on (the default), IsAttacking turning on raises CombatEvents.AttackStarted with Attack Threat Radius. Call NotifyAttackStarted yourself when you know the reach of the particular attack; it does not announce twice. MakeNoise(radius) raises a noise enemies can hear (shouts, landings, smashed pots).

In Play mode the component's inspector shows what enemies are reading: registered, alive, the four flags, recent dodges, speed and health.

Hitting enemies

Every enemy has a Health, so any hit is damageable.TakeDamage(info). The important part is DamageInfo.Source: an enemy that is hit looks for an ICombatTarget on the source object (or its parents), becomes alert to it and warns its group. Hits without a source still hurt, but the enemy does not know who to turn on. SimpleCombatTarget has two helpers that fill this in:

using System.Collections;
using TinnyStudios.AIUtility.RPGEnemies;
using UnityEngine;

public class MyPlayerCombat : MonoBehaviour
{
    public float Reach = 1.3f, Radius = 1.2f, Windup = 0.2f;
    private SimpleCombatTarget _combat;

    private void Awake() => _combat = GetComponent<SimpleCombatTarget>();

    private void Update()
    {
        _combat.IsBlocking = Input.GetMouseButton(1);          // any input backend works
        if (Input.GetMouseButtonDown(0) && !_combat.IsAttacking)
            StartCoroutine(Swing());
    }

    private IEnumerator Swing()
    {
        // Enemies inside the reach can react during the wind-up (an agile one may dodge).
        _combat.NotifyAttackStarted(Windup + 0.1f, Reach + Radius);
        yield return new WaitForSeconds(Windup);

        var hit = _combat.NewDamage(25f, poiseDamage: 15f, knockback: 1f);   // Source, team and direction filled in
        hit.GuardDamage = 40f;                                              // optional: drains a raised guard faster
        var report = _combat.DealDamageInFront(Reach, Radius, hit);        // sphere in front of the aim point, makes noise

        if (report.Blocked > 0 && report.Hits == 0)
            Debug.Log("A guard took it: flank the enemy, or use a heavier hit");
    }
}
  • NewDamage(amount, poiseDamage, knockback) returns a DamageInfo with Source, SourceTeam and Direction set. Add GuardDamage (how much guard it drains, 0 = same as the damage) or Unblockable for special moves.
  • DealDamageInFront(reach, radius, info, noiseRadius) damages every enemy in a sphere reach metres in front of the aim point, never the player, and makes a noise. DealDamageAt(center, radius, info, noiseRadius) does the same anywhere. Both return a HitReport.
  • For anything else, use the pieces directly: CombatUtility.DamageSphere(center, radius, info, alreadyHit, ignore) for areas (pass a HashSet<IDamageable> as alreadyHit to hit each target once across several frames; the overload DamageSphere(center, radius, info, layerMask, alreadyHit, ignore) checks only the layers you pass, such as a hurtbox layer), or CombatUtility.TryDamage(CombatUtility.GetDamageable(collider), info) for raycast weapons. Unlike calling TakeDamage directly, TryDamage checks the team rule and skips null and dead targets. GetDamageable is the lookup every hit in the pack uses: the damageable on the collider or a parent, remembered per collider, so use it instead of GetComponentInParent<IDamageable>() in hit code that runs every frame.
  • Layers: enemy hits, projectiles, sight and ground probes check every layer by default, so nothing needs setting up. With a hurtbox or ground layer of your own, narrow them so props and terrain are never even checked: Hit Layers on an Attack Data (melee, charge and area hits) and on a Projectile prefab, Sight Blockers on Enemy Senses, Ground Layers on the Enemy Animator and the Flying Enemy Mover.
  • Projectiles: instantiate any Projectile prefab and call Launch(velocity, damageInfo, ownerTransform); it ignores colliders under the owner and only hurts the opposing team.

Enemy guards: a guarding enemy (GuardAction) blocks hits from within its Block Angle (140°) in front, once the guard is up (Raise Time, 0.25 s). Blocked hits deal 1 - Damage Reduction (10%, 5% for the Heavy) and drain GuardDamage (or the damage) from its guard points; an empty guard breaks, the hit lands in full and the enemy is staggered for longer. Hits from the side or behind, and Unblockable hits, land in full. A hit that reaches the enemy's poise (Health.MaxPoise) staggers it (Enemy.StaggerDuration) and may make it drop its weapon.

Your own damage system

SimpleCombatTarget works with any IDamageable, not only Health. Implement IDamageable on your own health script (Team, IsAlive, transform and TakeDamage, which returns what happened: see EHitResult below) and, optionally, INormalizedHealth (HealthNormalized, 0-1) so enemies can tell how hurt the player is; without it the target reports 1 while alive. The target uses its Damageable Override field if set, else the Health on its object, else any other IDamageable on it; SetDamageable(damageable) points it at another from code, e.g. a plain C# object. Everything else (registration, the flags, attack warnings, footsteps, NewDamage) works the same. Health raises the Hurt, Blocked, Perfect Block and Died feedback moments for the pack's characters; your own system raises them with FeedbackHub.Play if you want them. The Code Extending guide's HealthBridge sample is a complete bridge.

Implementing ICombatTarget yourself

Only needed if SimpleCombatTarget does not fit (e.g. a target that is not a MonoBehaviour). The interface has eleven members: Transform, AimPoint (centre of mass, used for aiming and line of sight), Velocity, IsAlive, HealthNormalized, IsAttacking, IsHealing, IsDodging, IsBlocking, RecentDodgeCount and Damageable. Register with CombatTargets.Register(this) in OnEnable and CombatTargets.Unregister(this) in OnDisable, raise CombatEvents.RaiseAttackStarted(this, position, radius) when you attack and CombatEvents.RaiseNoise(position, radius, gameObject) for footsteps. If the target's Damageable is on a team the enemies cannot hurt when it registers (the Enemy team, by default), the registry logs a warning once, because enemies ignore a target their team may not hurt.

3.2 Health and IDamageable

IDamageable is anything that can be hit: Team, IsAlive, transform and EHitResult TakeDamage(DamageInfo). Enemies, the player and props all use it; Throwable implements it on the Neutral team so explosive barrels blow up when hit.

Health is the ready-made implementation and what every enemy uses:

Field Meaning
Team Player, Enemy or Neutral (see Teams). Defaults to Enemy.
MaxHealth Current health starts full. Normalized is 0-1.
MaxPoise, PoiseRegenDelay, PoiseRegenRate A hidden stagger meter. Each hit's PoiseDamage lowers it; at 0 Events.OnPoiseBroken fires and it refills. The demo player has 1000 poise so it is never staggered.
Invulnerable While true, hits are ignored (and raise the Evaded feedback moment). The demo sets it during dodges and god mode.
Events OnDamaged(DamageInfo), OnPoiseBroken(DamageInfo), OnHealed(float), OnBlocked(DamageInfo, EHitResult) whenever the interceptors make the hit anything but Hit (Blocked, PerfectBlocked or Ignored), OnDied.

TakeDamage runs in this order: dead → Ignored; invulnerable → Ignored; every IDamageInterceptor in turn may change the info, and the first PerfectBlocked or Ignored cancels the hit (see Blocking and parrying below); damage and poise are applied, a block's reduced damage included; OnDamaged; OnDied at zero health; OnPoiseBroken at zero poise. TakeDamage does not check teams: the pack's hits ask the team rule before calling it, and so does CombatUtility.TryDamage. Heal(amount) clamps to max and fires OnHealed; Heal(amount, false) skips the feedback moment (use it for regeneration every frame). ResetHealth() refills everything, e.g. on respawn. SetMaxHealthMultiplier(m) scales max health from its original value keeping the current percentage (difficulty profiles use it). TakeDamage, Awake and Update are virtual, for a Health subclass of your own.

3.3 Teams

ETeam has Player, Enemy and Neutral. Whether a hit lands is up to the team rule, CombatUtility.TeamRule, which every hit in the pack asks through CombatUtility.CanDamage(attacker, victim): melee and area attacks, projectiles, throwables, explosions and CombatUtility.TryDamage. The default rule (DefaultTeamRule) lands a hit if the victim is Neutral or the teams differ. So enemies (team Enemy) damage the player (team Player) and neutral props, never each other; explosions are Neutral-sourced and hurt everyone, the thrower included. SimpleCombatTarget enforces the Player team on its Health; anything else you make damageable chooses its own.

For factions or friendly fire, set CombatUtility.TeamRule to your own ITeamRule (CanDamage(attacker, victim)); teams beyond the three are numbers cast to ETeam, e.g. (ETeam)10, set from code. The rule is reset on entering Play mode, so set it at runtime. It also decides whom enemies fight: an enemy only targets a registered combat target whose Damageable its team may hurt (EnemySenses.IsHostile), so a faction picks its targets by the same rule.

3.4 DamageInfo, EHitResult and HitReport

DamageInfo (a struct) is everything a hit carries:

Field Meaning
Amount, PoiseDamage, Knockback Health lost, poise lost, metres the victim is pushed along Direction.
GuardDamage Guard points drained when blocked; 0 means use Amount. Heavy hits set it high to break guards.
Unblockable Cannot be blocked or parried; the victim must dodge. Enemies show a red warning flash for these.
IsProjectile Set by Projectile.
Point, Direction Where it landed and which way it travelled (used for knockback and effect orientation).

EHitResult is what the victim reports back: Hit (full damage), Blocked (reduced), PerfectBlocked (a parry: no damage) or Ignored (invulnerable, dead, or wrong team). Attackers react to it: an enemy whose attack is Blocked recoils and gets a longer recovery (AttackAction.BlockedRecoil), and one whose melee attack is PerfectBlocked is parried (Enemy.GetParried: a 1.5× stagger and, for disarmable enemies, a dropped weapon).

HitReport sums an attack that can hit several targets: Hits, Blocked, PerfectBlocked, Total, and Point of the first landed hit (HasPoint).

3.5 Blocking and parrying: IDamageInterceptor

Put a component implementing IDamageInterceptor on the same object as the Health. Health calls Intercept(ref DamageInfo info) before every hit. Change the info (reduce Amount, zero PoiseDamage) and return the result: Blocked applies the modified damage, PerfectBlocked or Ignored cancels it, Hit lets it through. Health fires Events.OnBlocked for any result but Hit, and the Blocked / Perfect Block feedback moments, for you.

A Health can have several interceptors, a guard and armour for example, and runs them all in order: the interceptor components on its object in component order (found in Awake), then any added from code with health.AddInterceptor(interceptor), such as a buff, a component on a child or a plain C# object (RemoveInterceptor takes one out, Interceptors lists them). Each sees the hit as the one before left it. The first to return PerfectBlocked or Ignored cancels the hit and the rest are skipped; a Blocked from any of them makes the hit a block. On an enemy the Enemy component is an interceptor (its guard blocks through it), so an armour component placed below it reduces what the guard let through; Enemy.Intercept and GuardAction.Block are virtual for rules of your own.

public class MyGuard : MonoBehaviour, IDamageInterceptor
{
    public SimpleCombatTarget Combat;
    public float BlockAngle = 150f, ParryWindow = 0.2f, BlockDamageReduction = 0.85f;
    private float _blockStarted = float.NegativeInfinity;

    public void RaiseGuard() { _blockStarted = Time.time; Combat.IsBlocking = true; }
    public void LowerGuard() => Combat.IsBlocking = false;

    public EHitResult Intercept(ref DamageInfo info)
    {
        if (!Combat.IsBlocking || info.Unblockable)
            return EHitResult.Hit;

        // Only hits from the front are blocked, so enemies that flank you get through.
        var towardsAttacker = info.Source != null ? info.Source.transform.position - transform.position : -info.Direction;
        if (Vector3.Angle(transform.forward, CombatUtility.Flat(towardsAttacker, transform.forward)) > BlockAngle * 0.5f)
            return EHitResult.Hit;

        if (Time.time - _blockStarted <= ParryWindow)
            return EHitResult.PerfectBlocked;      // no damage; a melee attacker is staggered and may drop its weapon

        info.Amount *= 1f - BlockDamageReduction;
        info.PoiseDamage = 0f;
        info.Knockback *= 0.3f;
        return EHitResult.Blocked;
    }
}

DemoPlayer.Intercept is the full version: a guard meter drained by GuardDamage, a guard break when it empties (the hit lands in full and the player is stunned for GuardBreakDuration), a re-arm delay so parry cannot be spammed, and HumanoidGuardPose driven through IGuardPoseSource so the character visibly raises its shield. The pose jolts back when the guard takes a hit: from the Health in its parents or, without one, from the character's Blocked and Perfect Block feedback moments. With neither, call PlayBlockJolt(result) yourself.

3.6 Reflecting projectiles: IProjectileReflector

Arrows, cannon shells, fireballs and boulders are Projectiles with Reflectable on. When one hits a collider whose parents have both an IDamageable and an IProjectileReflector, TryReflect(projectile, out newDirection) is asked first. Return true to send it back: the projectile now belongs to the reflector's team, its damage and poise damage are multiplied by ReflectDamageMultiplier (2 for fireballs and boulders, 3 for arrows, 5 for cannon shells), its speed by ReflectSpeedMultiplier, and it ignores the reflector from then on. projectile.Owner is the shooter (or the last reflector), so aiming back at it is one line:

public bool TryReflect(Projectile projectile, out Vector3 newDirection)
{
    newDirection = Vector3.zero;
    if (!Combat.IsBlocking)
        return false;

    var owner = projectile.Owner;
    newDirection = owner != null ? owner.position + Vector3.up - projectile.transform.position : -projectile.Velocity;
    return true;
}

3.7 CombatEvents

Two static events every enemy listens to:

  • AttackStarted(ICombatTarget attacker, Vector3 position, float radius): raised by SimpleCombatTarget when IsAttacking turns on or NotifyAttackStarted is called. Every enemy whose target is the attacker and is within radius + 2 m re-evaluates its actions immediately instead of at its next reaction interval.
  • Noise(Vector3 position, float radius, GameObject source): raised by attacks when they land (AttackData.NoiseRadius, 8 m by default, 12 for area attacks), explosions (3-5× the blast radius), thrown objects landing (8 m), alarms, and the player's footsteps and swings. An enemy within radius × HearingMultiplier that is not alert becomes suspicious and investigates the spot; an alert enemy that cannot see its target updates the last known position instead. Sleeping enemies react less.

3.8 Projectiles

Projectile moves with sphere casts (Radius), falls with Gravity, expires after Lifetime, and either damages what it hits or, with ExplosionRadius above 0, damages everything in that radius through CombatUtility.DamageSphere and makes noise. Fire one with Launch(velocity, damageInfo, ownerTransform); enemy attacks set DamageInfo.Attack so the impact plays that attack's cue. Its Feedback group holds Launch, Impact, Reflect and Expire cues. Child particle systems, trails and audio sources fade out instead of vanishing when it is destroyed (FeedbackUtility.DetachEffects). Subclass it for homing or bouncing shots: Steer(velocity, deltaTime) returns the velocity to fly with each frame (turn it towards a target), Impact can bounce by setting Velocity and not calling base, and Launch, TryReflect and Expire are virtual too. A subclass can also change the hit it carries (Damage) in flight. Update calls Advance(Time.deltaTime); override it to move the projectile on another clock. The Code Extending guide's HomingProjectile is an example. The pack's projectile prefabs are in Prefabs/Projectiles.

3.9 Weapons and throwables

WeaponItem is a weapon in the world: held in a socket or lying on the ground. Its DamageMultiplier, PoiseMultiplier and ReachBonus are plain numbers any combat system can read, and enemies use all three on their melee attacks, follow-ups included. An enemy adds its weapon's ReachBonus to their Hit Reach and Max Range (AttackData.WeaponReach), so a longer weapon hits further, and the weapon inspector's Length (m) tool grows the bonus with the weapon (see Authoring). The enemy scales their Damage and Poise Damage by the held weapon's multipliers over those of the weapon it was made with (AttackData.WeaponDamageScale / WeaponPoiseScale, Enemy.StartingWeaponDamageMultiplier), because its Attack Data are tuned for that weapon: its own weapon changes nothing, and a heavier one picked up hits harder. To arm an enemy from code as if it had been built with a weapon (a random loadout for a spawned enemy), call Enemy.SetStartingWeapon(weapon): that weapon's multipliers become the baseline. Enemy.EquipWeapon picks one up instead. The demo player multiplies its attack damage by them and fights at 40% damage unarmed. Equip(socket, holder) parents it to a hand socket using HeldPosition / HeldRotation, Drop(velocity) tosses it to the ground (Equip(socket, holder, raiseFeedback), which both Equip overloads end in, and Drop are virtual, with a protected virtual Land, for weapons of your own), WeaponItem.FindNearest(position, range) finds one to pick up, and Reserve(owner) / Release(owner) stop two characters running for the same one. Enemies drop theirs when parried, sometimes when staggered, and on death (Enemy.DropWeaponWhenParried, DropWeaponChanceOnStagger, DropWeaponOnDeath), then run for the nearest weapon or a WeaponRack (PickUpWeaponAction), which gives the player a window. The player may grab a weapon an enemy is running for.

Throwable is anything that can be picked up and lobbed: Reserve(owner), PickUp(holdPoint), then Throw(target, flightTime, damageInfo, thrower) for a ballistic arc that lands after flightTime seconds (or on the first character it hits). The DamageInfo is used whole: its Source and team, and any amount left at 0 takes the throwable's own Damage, PoiseDamage or Knockback. Explosive ones blow up on landing and when hit (on the Neutral team, so they hurt everyone, thrower included); others deal their damage in ImpactRadius to the thrower's enemies only and either BreakOnImpact (crates) or land and can be thrown again (rocks). Enemies use them with ThrowObjectAction; Throwable.FindNearest and Throwable.All are public for your own pick-up code, and PickUp, Throw, Land, Explode, TakeDamage and MakeImpactDamage are virtual.

3.10 Feedback: sounds and effects

Every gameplay moment the pack produces (a swing, a hit, a block, a footstep, an alert, a death, a wave starting: the full list is EFeedbackMoment, about 60 of them) is raised through one static hub and turned into sounds, particles, camera shake, hit-stop, colour flashes and light flashes. Three concepts:

  • Cue (FeedbackCue, plain data): what plays. Audio clips picked at random with pitch and volume variation, a pooled prefab at an anchor, camera shake, hit-stop, flash, light. Cues sit inline on the thing they belong to: every AttackData has Windup, Release and Impact cues, every action has Begin and End, PlayAnimationAction an Effect Cue, projectiles, throwables, weapons and spawners their own groups.
  • Profile (FeedbackProfile, one asset per character type): a list of (moment, optional key) → cue. Its Parent supplies anything it does not list, so a "Humanoid" base can serve many enemies. Keys target one action or attack by its Name or Display Name.
  • Emitter (FeedbackEmitter, one component per character or prop): holds the profile, finds anchors (head, hands, weapon, muzzle, FeedbackAnchor children), detects footsteps, and exposes every moment to the inspector (Responses, UnityEvents) and to code (Played).

Resolution, from FeedbackHub: a profile entry marked Override Inline → the cue set inline on the action, attack or prop → the character's profile (exact key first, then the general entry) → its parents → the scene FeedbackManager's Default Profile. A cue with Layering = Replace ends the search; Add keeps going, so an attack's extra crunch can layer on the character's generic hit sound. If nothing that played has a visual, the moment's built-in placeholder shows (a burst, a ground disc, a translucent shield), so a scene looks complete before any art exists; turn them off per profile (Placeholders: Never, on a profile of yours, such as the one the Feedback Emitter's Make Unique makes) or globally (FeedbackManager.ShowPlaceholders).

FeedbackManager is the built-in output and the scene's settings: pooled audio voices (MaxVoices, mixer group, 3D settings), pooled prefabs, camera shake (or ShakeRequested for your own camera), hit-stop (turn off for multiplayer), and DefaultProfile. One is created on first use; add one to the scene to change settings.

Where things go: voices and body sounds belong in the profile (they belong to the model, and the Swap Model tool can swap the profile with the model); weapon and spell sounds go on the attack or action (they belong to the move).

Footsteps come from the emitter without any component on the model: humanoids step when a foot bone plants, other walkers every stride, flyers never (Footsteps, Stride Length). Add animation events named Footstep to your own clips and that character switches to event-driven steps; events named Feedback(string key) and FeedbackMoment(int) raise custom or built-in moments. The receiver is added to the Animator's object at runtime, so it survives model swaps.

Your own player can raise the same moments so one profile dresses it: the demo player raises Attack Wind-up, Release, Impact and Blocked keyed "Light" or "Heavy", Dodge, Guard Raised / Lowered / Broken and Action Effect keyed "Heal" with FeedbackHub.Play(emitter, new FeedbackEvent(moment, transform, position) { Key = ..., Direction = ... }), and FeedbackHub.Begin / End for spans such as a wind-up. Health raises Hurt, Blocked, Perfect Block, Evaded, Healed and Died on any object, your player included. Implement IFeedbackMomentSource to list your moments in the emitter's checklist.

Middleware: implement IFeedbackOutput (Play(cue, event, placement) returning an id, Stop(id)) and assign it to FeedbackManager.CustomOutput, or set FeedbackHub.Output. Cues carry an AudioEvent string for FMOD or Wwise paths that the built-in output ignores.

4 Render Pipelines

RPG Enemies runs on the Built-in Render Pipeline as shipped and on the Universal Render Pipeline (URP) after a one-click conversion. HDRP is not supported (untested). Nothing in the AI, combat or feedback code depends on a pipeline; only the art does: the materials, the demo sky and the placeholder visuals.

4.1 Built-in Render Pipeline

Nothing to do. Import the pack, open Demo/Scenes/RPGEnemiesDemo.unity, press Play. The optional Post Processing package (com.unity.postprocessing) adds the demo's bloom, colour grading, ambient occlusion and vignette; without it the scene uses its lighting and fog only.

4.2 Universal Render Pipeline

The pack's materials use the Built-in Standard shader, which URP renders pink, so they have to be converted once:

  1. Install Universal RP (com.unity.render-pipelines.universal) and assign a URP asset in Project Settings ▸ Graphics (and in Quality, if a quality level overrides it). The Universal 3D project template does this for you.
  2. Import RPG Enemies. On the next editor load the pack notices that URP is active while its materials are still Built-in and offers the conversion. Choose Convert. To run it later (or again after re-importing the pack) use Tools ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Render Pipeline ▸ Convert Materials To URP. Check Materials in the same menu reports the active pipeline and how many materials are on each side.
  3. Open the demo scene and press Play.

Everything happens inside Assets/TinnyStudios/RPGEnemies, in place, and is logged to the Console as a per-file report. Revert Materials To Built-in maps everything back, so switching a project between the pipelines costs nothing. Both commands are safe to re-run: materials already on the target pipeline are left alone.

Post-processing on URP

The demo's post-processing was authored for the Built-in Post Processing package (PPv2), which does nothing on a Scriptable Render Pipeline. On URP the package dependency check no longer offers it, and the two PPv2 components in the demo scene (PostProcessLayer on the Main Camera, the Post Process Volume object) show as missing scripts if the package is not installed; remove them or ignore the warning. Post-processing is optional. To get the same look on URP:

  1. Add a Global Volume (GameObject ▸ Volume ▸ Global Volume) with a new profile and these overrides: Bloom (intensity 1.1, threshold 1.05, scatter 0.6), Color Adjustments (post exposure 0.15, contrast 16, saturation 14), Vignette (intensity 0.26, smoothness 0.42), and optionally Screen Space Ambient Occlusion as a Renderer Feature on the URP renderer (intensity 0.55).
  2. On the Main Camera tick Rendering ▸ Post Processing, and set anti-aliasing to SMAA on the camera (MSAA is set on the URP asset).

Building

Sprites/Default and the URP shaders are found by name at runtime; converted materials in your scenes keep the URP shaders in the build. If you strip shaders aggressively, keep Universal Render Pipeline/Unlit (or Sprites/Default) for the placeholders.

4.3 HDRP

Not supported and untested. The converter only targets URP; HDRP would need its own material conversion (Unity's Render Pipeline Converter can attempt the Standard materials), a Lit-compatible sky and an HDRP Volume. The runtime code itself does not depend on the pipeline.

4.4 Troubleshooting

  • Everything is pink on URP: the materials are still Built-in. Run Check Materials to confirm, then Convert Materials To URP.
  • "The URP shaders were not found": the URP package is not installed. Install it from the Package Manager and run the conversion again.
  • Everything is pink or invisible on Built-in after trying URP: run Revert Materials To Built-in. It also works when the URP package has already been removed (the materials' shaders show as missing until then).
  • Emission disappeared after clicking a material: both material inspectors derive the _EMISSION keyword from the material's global illumination flags; the converter sets them, and re-running the conversion repairs any material edited by hand.
  • The pack asked about URP but I said "Don't Ask Again": the choice is stored per project (UserSettings) and forgotten when you run either conversion from the menu.

5 Archetypes

What each of the eight enemies does, how it is put together, and which knobs are worth turning. Every archetype is a prefab in Prefabs/Enemies assembled from the same parts; the numbers below are the shipped values at Normal difficulty, with each enemy's own weapon. A weapon's damage and poise multipliers count relative to the one the enemy starts with, so they only change its hits when it picks up another. Turn the knobs on a Prefab Variant of your own, and on copies of the shared assets they name: Make Unique gives your enemy its own Attack Data, considerations and Feedback Profile, since a pack update replaces the pack's own (Authoring).

5.1 How every enemy works

An enemy prefab is one root object plus an Actions child holding one GameObject per behaviour:

  • Root: a collider, a NavMeshAgent with NavMeshEnemyMover (or a FlyingEnemyMover for the Swarm), Health, EnemySenses (sight cone, hearing, awareness), EnemyContext (the blackboard actions and considerations read), the core Agent and UtilityPlanner (re-evaluates every 0.25 s at Normal, keeps a 0.1 commitment bonus on the running action so enemies do not flip-flop), Enemy (hit reactions, stagger, death, phases, weapon), an animator driver and a FeedbackEmitter.
  • Actions: each child is one action with a Weight, an Interruptible flag, a Cooldown and a list of Considerations. The planner scores every action as weight × the product of its considerations and runs the best one. An AttackAction plays one AttackData asset (Data/Attacks), so attacks are data, not code.

6 Authoring

Making your own enemies and variants, new attacks, the animation workflow, weapons, putting your own models on the pack's enemies, and the generic building blocks for behaviours the pack does not have.

The Customization guide (RPGEnemies_Customization.pdf, next to this manual) walks through the no-code tasks step by step: your character on an enemy, a weapon's grip and size, replacing and adding animations, new attacks, sounds and effects, and tuning behaviour. This chapter is the reference behind it.

6.1 A variant in ten minutes

  1. Create a Prefab Variant of a prefab in Prefabs/Enemies and move it into your own folder. A variant keeps receiving the pack's changes, while anything edited inside the pack's folder is overwritten when you update the pack. (A variant plays its original's animation clips until you press Make Unique in its Clip Mapping, below.)
  2. On the root, change what the enemy is: Health (max health, poise), EnemySenses (sight range, field of view, hearing), Enemy (display name and archetype shown in the debug view, stagger duration, knockback multiplier, weapon drops, phase thresholds, body removal), the NavMeshAgent speed and radius, and which difficulty profile it uses (to tune one, duplicate it into your folder first: every enemy that uses it would change).
  3. Under Actions, change what it does. Each child is one action: delete the ones you do not want, duplicate one to add another, and edit its Weight, Interruptible, Cooldown Duration and Considerations. An attack is an AttackAction child with an AttackData asset in its Attack field; give it In Attack Range and (for melee) Can Take Attack Token considerations, plus whatever else should gate it. Attack Data and considerations are shared assets: before you tune one, give the action its own copy with Make Unique, under its Attack field (it offers to copy a combo's follow-ups too) or next to the consideration. It saves the copy next to your prefab and points the action at it.
  4. Press Play with the AI Debug View on (F1) and watch the scores. An action that never runs shows which consideration is holding it at zero; two actions that flicker need a higher UtilityPlanner ▸ Commitment Bonus, or a cooldown (an attack's is the Cooldown of its Attack Data: its own copy, from Make Unique).

Every tool that would change one of the pack's own prefabs (Make Unique, the Feedback Emitter's Create, Replace Weapon, Swap Model, a clip dropped in the Clip Mapping, the Behaviour Library's Add) asks first, "Change the pack's prefab?": Create Variant, the default, saves a Prefab Variant of it in your folder ("My Melee Soldier") and makes the change there; Change the pack's prefab does it anyway, until the next update. Scene objects and your own prefabs are changed without the question. Tools that would save a new file inside the pack's folders ask "Save in the pack's folder?" the same way, your folder by default. How often an enemy re-plans is its difficulty profile's Reaction Time: Enemy writes it into the planner's Reevaluate Interval when it starts and whenever its difficulty changes, so the interval set on the prefab is not used.

Actions added from code (AddComponent) work too: hand them to the enemy's Agent.AddAction, which sets them up (their Setup creates the serialized fields, then calls OnSetup) and ignores one it already has. Add their considerations with AddConsideration, before or after: the list is never null.

6.2 Considerations

Considerations are ScriptableObjects in Data/Considerations; reuse them across actions and enemies, or create new ones in your folder with Create ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Considerations:

Type What it scores
Enemy Flag A yes/no question from EEnemyFlag (has target, can see target, target is attacking / healing / dodging / blocking, has weapon, can take attack token, recently hit, is hidden, allies unaware, pending roar, throwable / weapon / rack nearby, ally needs healing, is facing target, ...). Invert flips it; True Score and False Score set the two values, so Punish Healing is 1 when healing and 0.5 otherwise rather than 0.
Enemy Value A number from EEnemyValue (awareness, health, distance to target, time since last hit / target seen / alert, time hidden, time exposed, allies nearby, target health, target recent dodges, minion count, phase, angle from the target's front) mapped from Min-Max to 0-1, shaped by the response curve, scaled to Min Score-Max Score. A steep curve over a tiny range makes a threshold: Hit In Last 0.8s maps 0.8 to 0.81 with a descending curve.
Attack Range 1 when the target is inside the action's own AttackData range, Edge Score at the edges, 0 outside; optionally requires line of sight. One asset works for every attack.
Phase 1 between Min Phase and Max Phase.
Composite Any takes the highest child score (an "or"), All multiplies them. An action's own list always multiplies, so use Any for "hide when hit OR approached OR exposed too long". Works with any consideration type.
Core: Distance Range, Health Threshold, Random Chance Work because EnemyContext implements the core ITargetDistanceContext and IHealthContext.

The flags and values themselves come from EnemyContext (Scripts/Runtime/Enemy/EnemyContext.cs). For a question they do not cover, write an EnemyConsideration subclass in your own folder. It casts the agent's context for you: override GetScore(EnemyContext context, IUtilityAction action), return 0-1 through GetSimulatedScore(value) so the Response Curve applies, and read whatever you like (the context, the action being scored, your game's state, a component of your own). One asset serves every enemy that lists it, so keep no per-enemy state in its fields. The Code Extending guide has worked examples. Do not add entries to the pack's EEnemyFlag or EEnemyValue: consideration assets store them by number, and a pack update replaces the file, so your entries are lost and your assets can end up asking a different question.

6.3 Attacks as data

Create one in your folder with Create ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Attack Data, or duplicate one of the pack's into your folder, and drop it into an AttackAction. To tune an attack an action already plays, press Make Unique under its Attack field instead: the action gets its own copy, named like "My Soldier Slash" and kept next to its prefab with the same Display Name, and for a combo it asks whether to copy the follow-ups too, chained the same way.

Section Fields
Type Melee (a hit sphere Hit Radius at Hit Reach in front, with an optional Lunge Distance), Projectile (Projectile Prefab, Projectile Speed, Projectile Count and Spread Angle, Lead Target), Area (Area Radius around the enemy, or around the target's position at the start of the wind-up with Area On Target), Charge (a dash at Charge Speed, Charge Overshoot metres past the target, hitting anything in the way).
Range Min Range-Max Range; the Attack Range consideration reads these. A Melee attack adds the held weapon's Reach Bonus to Max Range and Hit Reach, follow-ups included (see Weapons below).
Timing Windup (the telegraph; multiplied by the difficulty), Active, Recovery (the punish window), Cooldown (also scaled by difficulty), Track Target During Windup (off for attacks the player should sidestep).

For a new move, name a spare slot, Custom1-Custom6, or a state of your own (see Animation below). Or Windup State / Active State / Recover State for attacks built from three clips (charges use ChargeWindup, ChargeRun, ChargeRecover). Windup Anim / Active Anim are the fallbacks for characters without those states.

AttackAction itself has Blocked Recoil (extra recovery when the player blocks it). For an attack the pack cannot express (a beam, a grab, a multi-stage cast), subclass AttackData for its settings and AttackAction for its behaviour. For each attack of a combo the virtual hooks run in this order: StartAttack(data) (the wind-up); BeginActive() (sets ActiveDuration, then lunges, calls FireProjectiles(), damages the area or calls StartCharge(), and calls BeginReleaseFeedback()); UpdateActive() on every update of the active phase (melee and charge hits, each result through HandleReport(report)); BeginRecovery(); and PickFollowUp(), which returns the next Attack Data or null. MakeAttackDamage() builds every hit. Subclasses also get CurrentAttack, Phase, PhaseTime, ComboCount, WindupTime, a settable ActiveDuration and AreaCenter, HitThisAttack (clear it for an attack that hits again), HitReach, HitCenter and EnterPhase(phase). The Code Extending guide builds a beam this way.

6.4 Weapons: grip, size and swaps

A weapon is a WeaponItem. How it sits in a hand is its own Held Position and Held Rotation, in the hand socket's frame: the origin is the centre of the fist, +Z runs along the blade and +Y is up. Every Equip applies them, so moving a held weapon's transform by hand does not last. The inspector's Grip and Size section edits them and moves the held weapon with them:

  • Edit Grip: Scene-view handles at the fist (also in the Tools overlay) that turn the weapon around the fist and slide it through the hand. The fist is drawn with a blue "blade +Z" arrow, a green "up +Y" arrow and the tip's distance.
  • Flip turns a weapon held by its blade end for end. Grip From Mesh works the grip out from the mesh the way the pack's weapons were made, at Grip Along Length (0 the back end, 1 the tip).
  • Length (m) resizes the weapon around the fist, so the handle stays in the hand. With Adjust Reach on (the default), Reach Bonus grows by how much further the tip reaches.
  • Use Current Transform keeps a move made with the Transform tools, and Snap Back undoes it. Moves made while the weapon is selected are kept automatically, and the Scale tool resizes it around the fist, like Length.
  • Check Through An Animation poses the character in any state of its controller, its own attacks first: pick a State and press Pose. It starts at the hit frame; scrub, Loop / Pause, Hit, Stop. Nothing is saved or dirtied.

An enemy adds its weapon's Reach Bonus to the Hit Reach and Max Range of its melee attacks, follow-ups included. For example, lengthening the Melee Soldier's cleaver from 0.75 m to 0.975 m moves its tip from 0.66 m to 0.86 m in front of the fist, gives it a Reach Bonus of +0.198, and lands Soldier Slash 1.398 m ahead instead of 1.2 m.

The weapon's Damage Multiplier and Poise Multiplier scale the damage and poise damage of those attacks too, but relative to the weapon the enemy was made with, since its Attack Data are tuned for that one: with its own weapon it hits exactly as its Attack Data say, and a heavier weapon picked up hits harder. A Melee Soldier (built with the 1× cleaver) that grabs the demo's Heavy Club (1.4× damage, 1.8× poise) hits 1.4 times as hard, with 1.8 times the poise damage. The reach is not relative: set the ranges of your copy of an attack with its own weapon's Reach Bonus in mind.

Replace Weapon, under the weapon fields of the Enemy inspector (and the demo player's), gives a character a copy of any Weapon Item prefab: it goes into the same socket with its own grip, Weapon and anything else that named the old weapon are re-pointed, and the old one is deleted. On a prefab asset it opens Prefab Mode first, so the change can be undone there. Use it on your own variant: on one of the pack's prefabs, which the next update puts back, it asks first and offers Create Variant, which gives the weapon to a new variant in your folder. The new weapon is then the one the enemy starts with, so its multipliers become the baseline and its Reach Bonus counts in full. To change the grip or size of one of the pack's weapon prefabs, make a Prefab Variant of it in your folder and change that. Create Weapon From Model (select a model in the Project window, then Create ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Weapon From Selected Model, or Tools ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Create Weapon From Selected Model) makes a Weapon Item prefab from any model, with the grip worked out from its mesh.

6.5 Animation

The shared base Animator

Every rigged character uses the same Animation/EnemyBase.controller, so actions can play states by name on any character: a locomotion blend tree (Idle, Walking, Running, WalkBackward, StrafeLeft, StrafeRight, driven by MoveX / MoveZ / Speed), loops (SitIdle, SleepIdle, CrouchIdle, CarryIdle, CastChannel, HiddenPeek, ChargeRun, Guard), held poses (Death, TeleportOut, ChargeWindup, Hide), one-shots (Alert, GuardHit, Stagger, StaggerLong, Recoil, SpawnIn, PickUp, Taunt, BlowHorn, Roar, Throw, DodgeBack, DodgeSide, TeleportIn, PopUp, ChargeRecover, CastHeal, Summon), the generic attack phases (Windup, Attack, Recover), the named attacks (Punch, Kick, Slash1, Slash2, HeavySmash, GroundSlam, QuickSlash1-3, Lunge, Shoot, TripleShot, CastFireball, CannonShot, Sweep1-3, Slam, DelayedSmash, BoulderThrow, FireRing) and six spare slots, Custom1-Custom6, then any states of your own (below). An Upper Body layer (masked by Animation/EnemyUpperBody.mask) holds Guard and GuardHit so a guard stays up while the legs walk.

Each character gets an Animator Override Controller of the base that swaps in its own clips. Missing clips fall back to similar states (StaggerLong → Stagger, Taunt → Roar → BlowHorn → Alert, and so on: the pack's table of states in EnemyAnimatorTools.cs, which you read but never edit), and finally to Idle, so a character with five clips still plays everything.

A new move needs no code. Drop its clip on a spare slot, Custom1 to Custom6, in the character's Clip Mapping (below), and name the slot in an Attack Data's or a PlayAnimationAction's Animation State. Each character fills its own slots, so one Attack Data naming Custom1 plays whatever each character has there; an empty slot plays the character's generic Attack clip, or Idle. Hit frames are detected for the slots as for any attack.

A named state of your own needs no code either, for a move with a name (ShieldBash) or more than six new moves. In the Clip Mapping, Your states ▸ Add State… asks for its name (checked as you type), its kind (One shot, Loop or Hold), whether it is an attack, what it falls back to (for an attack, Its own attack by default: each character's first attack clip, from Attack, Slash1, QuickSlash1, HeavySmash, Sweep1, Punch, Kick, Shoot, CastFireball and CannonShot, then Idle), and whether the Upper Body layer gets it too. It goes into an ExtraAnimatorStates asset in your folder (Create ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Extra Animator States, made for you the first time) and into the base Animator, undoably; every character plays its fallback until it has a clip of its own, and [AnimatorState] menus list your states first. Tools ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Update Base Animator merges the pack's states with yours and gives every Override Controller a fallback clip for any state it lacks; the pack also runs it after every change to your asset.

Each state's empty placeholder clip is stored inside your asset, not in EnemyBase.controller, and every Override Controller keys its clip for the state by that placeholder. When a pack update replaces EnemyBase.controller, your states are put back with the same placeholders by themselves (the Console says so), so every character with its own Override Controller keeps its clip, and the pack's own characters play the fallback. Limits: a Hold state is added to Hold States only on the character you added it from; a state on the Upper Body layer plays until code calls StopUpperBody; and keeping the base in step removes from EnemyBase.controller any state without a clip that it did not add, which only matters for a hand edit to the pack's controller. The Code Extending guide takes one, a shield bash, from script to Play mode.

Building a character's animator from a folder of clips

  1. Name the clip files Prefix_Anim_State.fbx after the states above (Orc_Anim_Idle.fbx, Orc_Anim_Walking.fbx, Orc_Anim_Slash1.fbx, ...) and put them in one folder with the rigged model. A file may hold several clips cut from one take; any clip named after a state is used for that state. At least Prefix_Anim_Idle is required.
  2. Select the folder and run Tools ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Create Enemy Animator From Selected Folder. It configures the Humanoid clip imports for AI-driven characters (root motion baked into the pose, height from the feet, the folder's rigged avatar copied to clips exported without one; only clips never set up before are touched), creates Prefix.overrideController in the folder, and logs which states use fallbacks and which clip names matched nothing.
  3. Assign the controller to the model's Animator, add an EnemyAnimator to the enemy root pointing at that Animator, and press Detect Hit Frames in its inspector. It reads a Hit Animation Event from each attack clip when there is one (add it in the import settings of your own clips, Dont Require Receiver; for the pack's clips, whose import settings an update replaces, edit the Hit Markers instead), otherwise it samples the clip and takes the moment the weapon hand (or the foot, for kicks) moves fastest. The result is the Hit Markers list, editable by hand.
  4. Press Measure Stride Speeds: it times how fast each locomotion clip's feet move at this character's size (Walk, Run, Backward, Strafe Clip Speed) so clips play at exactly the speed the AI moves, and measures which states float or sink on this character (Ground Offsets). Ground Clips adjusts the clips' import height offsets so the feet (or the body, for clips that lie down) rest on the ground.

At runtime EnemyAnimator feeds the blend tree from the enemy's real movement, time-warps one-shot clips to the action's duration (between Min and Max Playback Speed), plays an attack clip so its hit frame lands when damage is dealt, holds Hold States (Death, Hide, ...) instead of returning to locomotion, snaps the model's soles to the real ground (Snap To Ground, off for flyers; Ground Layers narrows what counts as ground), lifts a fallen body so nothing sinks (Keep Above Ground States), and tints the renderers for telegraphs, hit flashes and difficulty (Tint). State Overrides remaps a general animation to another state.

Characters without a rig use ProceduralEnemyAnimator (bob, squash and tilt on a Visual transform, a Weapon Pivot swing); the Swarm's static model uses it. Both implement IEnemyAnimator, which actions and the mover drive: to use an animation setup of your own, implement it (the Code Extending guide's SimpleAnimatorDriver is an example) on the enemy or any child, in place of the Enemy Animator.

Clip Mapping: changing one clip

The Clip Mapping at the top of the EnemyAnimator inspector, under Swap Model, lists every state of the character's Override Controller with the clip it plays, grouped (Locomotion, Loops, Held poses, Reactions and one-shots, Attacks, Spare slots for your own moves) and filtered by Find State.

  • Drop a clip on a state to play it instead; any Humanoid clip retargets. A clip file that was never set up gets the folder tool's import setup; a one-shot (an attack, a spare slot, a reaction) gets its hit frame detected again, keeping the other Hit Markers; a locomotion clip gets the stride speeds measured again, and any other state its ground offset. The measuring needs a Humanoid. Clearing a state's clip puts it back on its fallback. Undoable, apart from the import setup.
  • Two markers, named by a legend above Find State. ⚠ Fallback (no clip of its own): the state has no clip, or exactly the clip its fallbacks fill it with. A link icon, Borrowed through State Overrides: this Enemy Animator's State Overrides play another state for the general animation that uses this one. Where a clip came from decides it, not its name: a clip you dropped is never marked, and neither is an empty spare slot.
  • ▶ poses the character in that state in the Scene view, at the hit frame, with a time line to scrub (Loop / Pause, Hit, Stop). It uses the real clip in Edit mode, like the Animation window, and nothing is saved or dirtied.
  • Shared controllers. When other prefabs play the same Override Controller, a warning names them and Make Unique copies the controller next to this character's prefab and assigns the copy; on one of the pack's own prefabs it offers Create Variant first, and gives the copy to a new variant in your folder. A Prefab Variant shares its original's controller until it is made unique. A clip dropped on a shared controller asks first: Make Unique and replace, Change it for all (not asked again for that controller until Unity restarts), or Cancel; one dropped on one of the pack's animation sets that no other prefab plays asks "Change the pack's clip?" the same way.

In the EnemyAnimator inspector, the Swap Model section replaces the model inside an enemy while keeping everything else: the AI, actions, considerations, attacks, weapon, shield and guard stance. Swap Model… at the top of the Enemy inspector opens the same section in a window of its own, which follows the selection. What you pick is kept per character and cleared after a swap.

HumanoidGuardPose: guarding without a guard clip

Enemies and the demo player play the Guard and GuardHit clips on the Upper Body layer. A Humanoid character with no Guard clip can use HumanoidGuardPose instead: add it to the model next to the Animator (the base layer's IK pass is already on) and it layers a procedural stance on whatever plays: knees bend, the guarding shoulder leads, the left hand brings a shield (Shield, optional) up in front of the chest, the weapon is held low and ready (Shield style) or raised across the body (Weapon style), and the head watches the threat. Hand targets are measured in arm lengths, so one setting suits any proportions; Use Style Defaults in the component menu resets them after changing Style. It finds its IGuardPoseSource (an Enemy, or your player implementing IsGuardRaised and GuardLookTarget) in the parents, and jolts back when the guard takes a hit: from the parent Health's blocks or, without one, from the character's Blocked and Perfect Block feedback moments (PlayBlockJolt(result) jolts it from code). Tick Preview Raised in Play mode to tune it. With neither a Guard clip nor a guard pose, a guarding enemy shows a translucent shield placeholder so the guard still reads.

6.6 Generic building blocks

PlayAnimationAction: stop, play an Animator state (Animation State, or Fallback Animation when the character lacks it) fitted to Duration while facing the target, optionally telegraphed with Effect Color, and fire an effect at Effect Time (a fraction of the duration; 1 means interrupting the action cancels it): a FeedbackCue (Effect Cue) and a UnityEvent (On Effect). Require Target is None, To Start or Throughout (an alarm the player can interrupt by escaping); Invulnerable ignores damage while playing. Taunt, Roar, Alert Allies, Summon, Heal Ally, Hide and Emerge are presets of it, so a victory dance or a war cry is just this component with a state name, and a new "cast, then something happens" move is a subclass overriding PerformEffect (plus OnPlay to pick a target or fail, IsStillValid, EffectPoint, OnStop).

RepositionAction (and its base PositionQueryAction): move to the best-scoring point around the target, found by the PositionPicker from a PositionQuery: rings of points between Min and Max Distance, scored by line of sight (Require for a clear shot, Avoid for cover), High Ground Bonus, Ally Spacing and Travel Penalty within Max Travel Distance; Require Hidden rejects a best point the target can still see. Then optionally hold there for Hold Duration seconds playing Hold State, recovering Heal Per Second, and leave if the target comes within Break Hold Distance. Find Clear Shot, Seek High Ground, Take Cover (the preset with Avoid, Require Hidden and a hold), Flee (runs) and Teleport (warps) are all this. Select the enemy in Play mode to see the scored points (red poor, green best, cyan ring chosen).

CompositeConsideration: the "or" (see Considerations above). The Pop-up Shooter's Should Hide is the example: Any of (hit in the last 0.8 s, target within 4 m, All of (has target, exposed over 6 s)).

Writing a new action: derive from EnemyAction and implement OnActionBegin, OnActionUpdate (return Running, Completed or Failed) and OnActionEnd, and OnSetup to cache things once Ctx is ready (Setup itself is sealed). You get Ctx (the context), Enemy, Mover (MoveTo, Dash, Warp, Stop, LookTarget), Senses, Coordinator, Self, Difficulty, Elapsed, and helpers: FaceTarget, MakeDamage, TryFindPointInDirection, MoveAwayFromTarget, SetInvulnerable, PlayState / PlayAnim, PlayUpperBody / StopUpperBody (on animators that layer the upper body), SetTelegraph, and PlayFeedback / BeginFeedback / EndFeedback for sounds and effects. Call FailOnNextUpdate() from OnActionBegin when the action cannot start (nothing to pick up, no spot to go to); CanRun adds code checks before scoring. Actions live on child objects of the enemy, so always move and rotate Self, never the action's own transform. Create ▸ TinnyStudios ▸ RPG Enemies ▸ Scripting ▸ Enemy Action writes a working one to start from, in your folder (there are Play Animation Action, Attack Action and Enemy Consideration templates too). The Behaviour Library lists it under Your action types as soon as it compiles, and Add puts it on your enemy; [BehaviourRole("…")] on the class picks its group, and [Description("…")] its text. The Code Extending guide (RPGEnemies_CodeExtending.pdf) takes one from the script to Play mode, and walks through a complete action.

6.7 Difficulty, groups and encounters

Difficulty profiles, GroupCoordinator and EnemySpawner are covered in Quick Start. Two authoring notes:

  • Enemy.ApplyDifficulty scales max health, awareness and reaction time, sets the mover's Difficulty speed modifier, and asks its group for the profile's Extra Attackers (the group allows the most any member asks for); AttackAction reads the damage, wind-up, cooldown and combo multipliers itself, and DodgeAction / GuardAction roll their Skill against the profile's chances. A profile is therefore safe to switch mid-fight. ApplyDifficulty is virtual, for stats of your own from a DifficultyProfile subclass, and the static Enemy.Spawned event is raised after it for every enemy that starts, so a difficulty menu can reach enemies that appear later.
  • An enemy joins the GroupCoordinator on its nearest parent when it initialises. Spawners parent what they spawn to themselves, and summons join the summoner's group. enemy.JoinGroup(group) moves an enemy to another group later.
